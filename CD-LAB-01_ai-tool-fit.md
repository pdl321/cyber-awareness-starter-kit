| Decision point | Proposed approach for BBR Academy |
| --- | --- |
| **One task where an AI draft may help** | Draft a reply to a learner asking when applications close, using the academy’s approved program information. |
| **One choice AI must not make on its own** | An authorised staff member decides whether the final reply is ready to send. |
| **Data that should not go to the AI provider** | Learner names, contact details, IDs, grades, identifiable case histories, health or payment information, private staff notes, credentials and raw logs. Send only the question with personal details removed and the relevant approved program excerpts. |
| **How a person would check the draft** | Confirm it answers the question, then verify the deadline, program and any links against the current approved source. Check for private information and unsupported claims. Correct errors or refer unresolved questions to the program owner before sending. |
| **One risk I would mitigate** | Reduce the risk of exposing learner information to the AI provider. Have the API check outgoing content for personal details and route cases to staff when those details cannot be safely removed. |
| **Why I chose that action and who could be affected** | The AI can draft a deadline reply without knowing the learner’s identity. Disclosure could affect the learner, other people named in the question or staff whose notes were included. |
| **Who owns the safeguard and who can approve the decision** | The service owner is accountable for the safeguard, and the API lead implements it. The academy’s designated risk owner approves any remaining risk after review by privacy and security staff. |
| **What risk remains and when staff must escalate** | Checks can miss identifying details in free text. If staff spot private information before transmission, hold the request for manual handling. If they suspect it already reached the provider, escalate immediately to security and the privacy lead. |
